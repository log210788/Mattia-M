## 1. Core Structure

| Form | Formula | Auxiliary by Subject |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Positive (+)** | `Subject + have / has + past participle` | **I / You / We / They** → `have` (`'ve`)<br>**He / She / It** → `has` (`'s`) |
| **Negative (-)** | `Subject + haven't / hasn't + past participle` | **I / You / We / They** → `haven't`<br>**He / She / It** → `hasn't` |
| **Question (?)** | `Have / Has + Subject + (ever) + past participle?` | **Have** `you / they ...?`<br>**Has** `he / she / it ...?` |

### Examples
- **Positive:**
  - *I **have visited** Paris.*
  - *She **has finished** work.*
  - *He **has already taken** the test. You don't need to remind him.*
  - *She **has made** a beautiful cake for the party.*
- **Negative:**
  - *I **haven't been** to Greece yet.*
  - *I **haven't visited** America yet.*
  - *He **hasn't called** yet.*
  - *They **haven't been** to Disneyland before.*
  - *(Note: For life experience, never say "I didn't go" — use **I haven't been**.)*
- **Question:**
  - ***Have** you **ever been** to London?*
  - ***Have** they **ever played** in a championship?*
  - ***Has** it **stopped** raining **yet**?*

## 2. Present Perfect vs. Past Simple ("When?")

- Use **Present Perfect** when the time is **unspecified** (general life experience) or **ongoing**:
  - ✅ *I **have visited** Paris.* (no specific time)
  - ✅ *I **have been** to France four times.*
- Use **Past Simple** the moment you state a **finished past time** (*last year, last summer, last month, in 2016, ten years ago*):
  - ✅ *I **visited** Paris **last year**.*
  - ✅ *I **went** to London **ten years ago** on a school trip.*
  - ❌ *I ~~have visited~~ Paris last summer.*

---

## 3. *Been* vs. *Gone*

- **`has been to`** → visited **and returned**:
  - ✅ *Amy **has been to** London. She loved it and she's home now.*
- **`has gone to`** → went **and is still there** (has not returned yet):
  - ✅ *James **has gone to** London.*
- ❌ *Amy ~~has went~~ to London.* (Never use `has went`!)

---

## 4. Key Time Markers

- **`for`** + **duration** (how long):
  - *She has driven **for five hours**.*
  - *I have worked in this industry **for three years**.*
- **`since`** + **specific starting point** (when it started):
  - *She has been sick **since last Monday**.*
  - *I have worked in this industry **since 2024**.*
- **`already`** → sooner than expected (positive sentences, before the past participle):
  - *I have **already** visited Tower Bridge.*
- **`yet`** → until now (questions & negatives, at the **end** of the sentence):
  - *Has it stopped raining **yet**?* / *I haven't visited America **yet**.*

---

## 5. Life Experience Frequency (*once, twice, three times*)

Place the frequency word **directly** after the experience — do **not** add `for`:
- ✅ *I have eaten sushi **once**.* (not *"for once"*)
- ✅ *I have been to London **twice**.*
- ✅ *I have been to Rome **three times**.*
- ✅ *I have been to France **four times**.*
