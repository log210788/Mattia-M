Trial Lesson Grammar Reference • Present Perfect

Present Perfect: Structure, Rules & Real Examples

Your complete template from today's lesson—covering positive, negative, and question forms, been vs. gone, for vs. since, and when to switch to Past Simple.

1. POSITIVE (+)

Subject + have / has + Past Participle

Use have ('ve) with I, you, we, they and has ('s) with he, she, it.

  • • I have visited Paris.
  • • She has finished work.
  • • He has already taken the test.
2. NEGATIVE (-)

Subject + haven't / hasn't + Past Participle

Never use "I didn't go" for general life experience—use haven't / hasn't + past participle.

  • • I haven't been to Greece yet.
  • • He hasn't called yet.
  • • They haven't been to Disneyland before.
3. QUESTION (?)

Have / Has + Subject + (ever) + Past Participle?

Use ever to ask about life experience; use yet at the end to ask if something expected has happened.

  • • Have you ever been to London?
  • • Has it stopped raining yet?
  • • Have they ever played in a championship?

Present Perfect vs. Past Simple ("When?")

Use Present Perfect when the time is not stated or is still ongoing. The moment you add a finished past time (last year, last summer, in 2016, ten years ago), switch to Past Simple.

✅ Present Perfect: "I have visited Paris." (Life experience — no specific time)

✅ Past Simple: "I visited Paris last year." (Specific finished time)

❌ Avoid: "I have visited Paris last year."

Been vs. Gone

Both are past participles of go, but they tell the listener where the person is now.

✅ has been to: "Amy has been to London. She loved it and she's home now." (Visited and returned)

✅ has gone to: "James has gone to London." (He is still there / hasn't returned yet)

❌ Never say: "Amy has went to London."

Time Markers: For, Since, Already, Yet

  • • for + duration (how long): "She has driven for five hours." / "I have worked here for three years."
  • • since + starting point (when it started): "She has been sick since last Monday." / "I have worked here since 2024."
  • • already (sooner than expected, positive): "I have already visited Tower Bridge."
  • • yet (until now, questions & negatives, at the end): "Has it stopped raining yet?" / "I haven't visited America yet."

Life Experience Frequency & Irregular Participles

State the number directly after the experience—do not add "for":

• "I have eaten sushi once." (1×)

• "I have been to London twice." (2×)

• "I have been to Rome three times." / "I have been to France four times." (3+×)

Irregulars from today: go → gone/been • drive → driven • run → run • make → made • take → taken • eat → eaten • see → seen
